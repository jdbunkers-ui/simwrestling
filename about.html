About the simulation

Understanding Wrestler Profiles

Each summary combines several hidden ratings into a readable scouting category. The individual inputs remain private so a wrestler must be evaluated through performance as well as reputation.

01

Athleticism

Power, explosiveness, quickness, balance and flexibility. It describes the physical tools a wrestler can apply during an exchange.

02

Conditioning

Endurance, recovery, toughness and weight-management resilience. It shapes how well performance holds up as energy falls.

03

Neutral Offense

Stance and motion, hand fighting, setups, leg entries, takedown finishing and upper-body offense.

04

Neutral Defense

Takedown defense, counters, upper-body defense, balance, awareness and wrestling near the edge.

05

Top Wrestling

Breakdowns, riding, turns, pinning and mat returns. It represents the ability to control and create bonus points.

06

Bottom Wrestling

Base building, escapes, reversals, mat-return defense and pin defense. It measures the ability to become safe and regain freedom.

07

Scrambling

Reactions, balance, flexibility, awareness and toughness when neither wrestler has clean control.

08

Wrestling IQ

Mat awareness, composure, consistency, discipline, judgment and the instinct to capitalize on scoring opportunities.

Year-over-year careers

Growth and Development

High-school wrestlers enter the simulation at different stages of readiness. Some are immediately advanced; others may improve dramatically over several seasons. Height, natural weight, technique and physical ability can all change as a career progresses.

Development is intentionally uneven and uncertain. Public scouting bands describe current ability only—they do not reveal a wrestler's potential, development speed or future areas of improvement.

Coach analytics glossary

How Performance Metrics Work

Coach Analytics is calculated from completed match events. Rates describe what happened—not a hidden attribute rating—and become more reliable as the wrestler accumulates matches.

Performance snapshot

TD conversion
Takedowns divided by takedown attempts.
TD defense
Opponent takedown attempts stopped, divided by attempts faced.
Turn rate
Successful turns divided by turn attempts.
Scramble rate
Scramble wins divided by scrambles entered.
Average margin
Average points scored minus points allowed per match.

Neutral and mat wrestling

Attack profile
Conversion rates for open-neutral, clinch, counter and throw attempts.
Breakdown rate
Successful breakdowns divided by breakdown attempts.
Ride / match
Average seconds of riding control per completed match.
Escape / reversal rate
Successful bottom-position outcomes divided by their attempts.
Danger time
Seconds spent exposing an opponent to near-fall or pin danger.

Move intelligence

Uses
Times the wrestler attempted the move.
Success
Successful resolutions divided by uses.
Points
Total scoring points directly created by the move.
Faced / Stops
Opponent attempts faced and the number successfully defended.
Stop %
Stops divided by attempts faced. Setup and positioning actions show N/A because they are not resolved scoring attempts.

Situational splits

Period scoring
Points for and against, takedowns and riding time within each period.
Score state
Production while leading, tied or trailing.
Fatigue band
Scoring-action rate and action volume at different energy levels.
Scramble control
Scrambles entered, wins and exchanges beginning from neutral.
Discipline
Stall warnings, warnings drawn and penalty points conceded.

Scouting language

Rating Bands

The ranges become narrower near the top because differences between elite wrestlers matter more. The public sees the band—not the exact composite.

Mastery95–100

Rare, defining capability at the highest level.

Exceptional88–94

A genuine competitive advantage against strong opposition.

High Level80–87

Reliable strength that consistently influences matches.

Advanced71–79

Clearly developed and effective in college competition.

Above Average61–70

Better than the typical wrestler, with room to become a major strength.

Solid51–60

Dependable and functional without creating a major advantage.

Average41–50

Competitive baseline performance for the current level.

Below Average31–40

A weakness that stronger opponents may be able to target.

Developing21–30

Early-stage skill or physical development requiring attention.

Limited11–20

Currently restricts the wrestler's tactical options.

Novice1–10

Minimal current capability at this competition level.
